About phasing out DSA interviews? I think it will take some time, and it will depend on a lot of factors. Here’s my take.

- Replacing an existing method entirely is not going to happen. For the next few years, you will see DSA interviews for sure. After some years, they will look at the signals and then decide.

- The curriculum in schools/colleges needs to be updated. Unless that happens, you can’t get into a college and ask something that no one is aware of, so it’s dependent on that as well.

- We at @takeUforward_ are building an AI assessment platform as well. From what we can figure out, it’s easy to sail through it if you have ever built something with AI. Obviously, with time, it’s going to evolve, but then you have a cap of 60-90 minutes in an interview, so that needs to be kept in check in respect to the difficulty bar. It works now, because less people are aware if it. The game starts when people prep for it.

- Companies need rounds that filter out the masses. They don’t look for the best, they look for people who fit the job description. With something like an AI assessment, it will take a maximum of 10-15 days of learning (keep in mind that there’s only a level up to which you can evaluate in a 60-90 minute AI assessment) to help people clear it.

- You can’t deny the fact that there will be an industry that will prepare people for the same. Historically, this has always happened. How do you deal with masses clearing it? How do you cut off false positives? With DSA, it took a few months, and not many were that consistent.

- With AI assessments, you introduce one more problem to solve - cheating. It’s a game of cat and mouse. The platform has a safety net, the cheaters bypass it. It keeps going on forever.

- Also, the company promoting it
aggressively lost against LC back in the day, so I feel it’s their bet to be the first mover this time. It’s a good thing that they are moving fast.

I will repeat, at scale, interviews are about filtering people who don’t clear the bar, and not about getting the best one, which means you need methods that are tough to manipulate/prepare for.

New things have come up always, and they have always been added to the process instead of replacing something. With time, the number of rounds has only grown, and it will keep growing, with more people clearing the initial rounds with ease.

Companies don’t run on internet arguments, they do what’s best for them.

It’s an ecosystem, for one thing to change, 10 other things need to align.

Why are interns hired in MNCs and startups?

A lot of people think internships are only for student learning. That is not true. Internships are also a business decision.

MNCs (2-month internships)
- Engineers are usually busy with tougher and more critical work
- Interns are often used for smaller tasks, POCs, or experiments

MNCs (6-month internships)
- The company gets more time to train and evaluate the intern
- It reduces the cost of onboarding for a future full-time hire
- It also acts like an extended probation period before giving a PPO

Startups
- Interns help with smaller but important tasks
- Sometimes there is no immediate full-time requirement
- It helps founders and teams test people before making a hiring decision
- It also reduces the risk and cost of wrong hires

At the end of the day, internships are not just about giving opportunities. They help companies solve problems, test talent, and manage hiring risk better.

That is why companies hire interns. It is good for students, but it also makes strong business sense for the company. With AI in the picture, few months gone in a wrong hire, will mean you are chasing your competitors.

18 Mar 2026

Currently we are figuring out how to manage analytics in-house for around 100M events a month.

From the outside, analytics looks simple. Track events, store them, query them. But when you actually sit down to build or even evaluate it at scale, reality hits differently.

PostHog is there. Good product. But self-hosting it at our scale needs a big machine and serious infra. Paid tools are convenient, but expensive at scale. Some cheaper options exist, but they do not give us the kind of per-user tracking we need to create business funnels.

As a bootstrapped company, you cannot just say, “Let’s buy this tool.” You have to ask:

- Do we really need everything it offers?
- Can we build only the limited parts we actually need?
- Can we keep the storage cheaper?
- Can we work with a TTL of 1–2 years instead of keeping everything forever?

Reading. Discussing. Trying. Failing. Reworking. Learning again. A lot of people think bootstrapped companies move slower because they spend too much time thinking about cost.

I think the opposite.

- Cost makes you think deeper.
- It forces you to understand the system properly.
- It pushes you to separate what is essential from what is just nice to have.

We are still figuring it out. All of us are learning on the go. But that is also the beauty of building this way.

When you cannot throw money at every problem because you don't have enough, you learn to trade-off.

Glad that we took a call to build a end to end prep platform, instead of a typical course platform hosted on some third party site.

⚠️ What happened at Cloudflare yesterday?

- Around 11:20 UTC, a huge portion of the internet started failing with 5xx errors.

- At first, Cloudflare thought it was a DDoS attack, because traffic patterns looked abnormal.

- But the real issue was internal - a config file suddenly doubled in size because duplicate entries were generated in their Bot Management system.

- This oversized file broke a core proxy component that handles traffic across Cloudflare’s network.

- As a result, dashboards, CDN traffic, security features - everything started failing.

- Fixing the config + rolling it out globally took a few hours, and things stabilized by ~17:00 UTC.

A small internal data bug → huge file → crashed a critical service → global outage.

Full read: blog.cloudflare.com/18-november-2025-outage

22 Oct 2025

Learnings from building the Payment Service from scratch 💳

- Don’t build only for plans, build for individual plan entities
- Support multiple validity periods
- Use Adapter Pattern to switch gateways easily
- Log every transaction, all detail's
- Build everything around transaction_id
- Handle upgrades & external product sales
- Future-proof for GST / tax changes
- Add convenience fee support
- Ensure idempotency (esp. webhooks & retries)
- Make external calls async to avoid blocking

Sharing this midway, will share more once done.

DSA was never about memorising.

The smartest ones don’t just “solve” problems, they spot patterns, build intuition, debug deeply, and think in edge cases.

Developers will relate, below given are the same steps, just played out in a larger, more complex environment.

- Navigating large codebases
- Understanding design patterns
- Debugging with log lines
- Thinking of edge cases of journeys

It all starts with those baby steps of thinking deeply, not blindly solving.

Memorizing might get you through interviews, but it won’t help when you’re dropped into a complex codebase. Get your fundamentals strong!
