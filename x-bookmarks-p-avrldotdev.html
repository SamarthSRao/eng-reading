  • 18 Apr 2026
    It's embarrassing to see people fighting over choosing Go or Java.
    So please decide based on this:

    > Go
    Throughput: Solid, predictable
    Latency: Low, minimal GC pauses
    Concurrency: goroutines, simple & easy
    Ecosystem: Lean, standard library first
    Startup Time: Instant

    > Java
    Throughput: High, tuned with JVM + JIT
    Latency: GC can bite under pressure
    Concurrency: Threads, mature but heavier
    Ecosystem: Massive (Spring dominates)
    Startup Time: Slow (JVM warmup)

  • 15 Apr 2026
    Algorithms ranked by the pain & tears they'll bring you for not knowing as a backend dev:

    0. Hashing – every lookup becomes a nightmare

    1. Binary Search – Inefficient queries & slow performance

    2. Sorting Algorithms – No sorting, no BS, no optimisation.

    3. Recursion/Divide-and-Conquer – struggle with efficient resource allocation

    4. Dynamic Programming – recompute will silently consume the whole server.

    5. Graph Algorithms (BFS/DFS) – cooked workflows & resource routing.

    6. Heap & Priority Queue – no rate limiting & broken message queues.

    7. Trie – slow searching & autocomplete

    8. Greedy Algorithms – cry in huge compute bill & cooked resource allocations

    9. Topological Sort – circular dependency eats your CPU

  • 7 Apr 2026
    As a college student or a fresher, there's no better time to contribute to OSS projects.

    0. You'd use AI to understand the codebase you want to contribute to.

    1. Take 2 hours & have a QnA session with AI creating a visual map of the codebase.

    2. Then take 5 merged PRs & ask AI questions to it on how the code is written in that repository. Make notes of it.

    3. After this, then you're mostly ready to contribute.

    4. Pick an Issue, read it, try to find the place where you have to start & you can ask AI to help you guide to the right place.

    5. Try to make the changes, use AI to review it.

    6. Now, go ahead & write the tests for your changes. Make sure you cover edge cases.

    7. Create a PR with a video demo & clear details on how you approached the changes.

    8. Resolve the review comments, if any & be patient. It can take days to weeks to get it merged.
    (Mine took a month to get merged).

    9. IMPORTANT
    Please don't vibe code & contribute, it's bad & don't do it ever.
    Use AI to learn & understand the code not just let it rip through everything.

    DON'T DO IT!
    You'll just be another nuisance creator for maintainers, & will gain bad rep for your vibing.

    Hope this helps!
