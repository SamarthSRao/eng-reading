  • 19 Aug 2026
    play factorio.

    seriously. it’s one of the best games for developing an engineering brain.

    you start with almost nothing and slowly build an automated industrial system.

    • automation → if you’re doing something repeatedly, build a machine for it
    • throughput → production is only as fast as the slowest bottleneck
    • logistics → materials need to arrive at the right place, at the right rate
    • scaling → something that works at 10 units/minute completely breaks at 1,000
    • feedback → shortages propagate through the entire factory
    • modularity → good factories are built from reusable production blocks
    • optimization → better layouts, shorter paths, higher utilization, less waste

    then the factory gets large enough that you stop thinking about individual machines.

    you start thinking about flows.

    • iron comes in → intermediate products move
    • through production → finished goods come out.

    you’re basically learning manufacturing, supply chains, automation, operations research, and systems engineering while playing a game.

    factorio teaches one idea extremely well:

    don’t do the work.
    engineer the system that does the work.

    patterns I’ve noticed in great engineers:

    • they think in systems, not events
    • they reduce problems before trying to solve them
    • they obsess over fundamentals more than tools
    • they can explain complex things without sounding clever
    • they build small, test early, break things on purpose
    • they respect physics, constraints, and reality
    • they document their thinking, not just results
    • they care about edge cases because that’s where truth hides
    • they borrow ideas across disciplines without ego
    • they have taste, and they know when something feels wrong
    • they value reliability over novelty
    • they keep learning long after they’re “qualified”

    none of this is flashy.
    none of this trends well online.

    but this is what quietly compounds
    into real mastery over time.

  • 1 Oct 2025
    phases of character development in engineering:

    phase 1 → arrogance. you think you’re smarter than everyone because you wrote a working “hello world”

    phase 2 → humility. your code breaks, your circuit burns, your robot refuses to move. you realize you don’t know anything

    phase 3 → patience. debugging for 6 hours teaches you that frustration doesn’t solve problems. persistence does

    phase 4 → discipline. you stop chasing shiny tutorials, and start building consistent habits

    phase 5 → responsibility. your design fails? your fault. no excuses. you own it, fix it, and move forward

    phase 6 → vision. you stop solving problems just for yourself. you start thinking about impact, scalability, legacy

    phase 7 → mastery. not just of tools, but of self. you know when to push, when to rest, and when to reinvent

    engineering doesn’t just build machines. it builds you

  • 1 Oct 2025
    phases of learning in engineering:

    phase 1 → copy-paste tutorials. you don’t understand a thing, but it works. feels like magic

    phase 2 → you start to break stuff on purpose. “what if i change this variable?” 90% failure, 10% breakthroughs

    phase 3 → you finally understand the underlying math, physics, and logic. it’s not magic anymore, it’s systems

    phase 4 → you start building your own projects. no guide, no youtube video. just you, datasheets, and google at 3am

    phase 5 → you realize engineering is less about knowing everything, and more about figuring it out. fast

    phase 6 → you loop back. new domain, same cycle. beginner again

    that’s the grind. that’s how you level up
