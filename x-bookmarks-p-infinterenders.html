  • 4 Nov 2026
    I hate writing code. Not because I dislike computer science, because manually translating ideas into instructions feels like solving the least interesting part of the problem. also I’m lazy asf too

    Programming, at its deepest level, is thinking in constraints.

    People confuse describing computation with executing computation. A program can be syntactically elegant, mathematically correct and architecturally sophisticated while still inducing an unnecessarily expensive execution.

    A few lines can trigger millions of memory accesses, allocations, branches and synchronization events. The real cost is governed by computational complexity, memory hierarchy, communication complexity and dependency structure, not by how concise the source code looks.

    This is also why human-crafted code isn't inherently superior to AI-generated code. Humans routinely produce software through pattern imitation, accumulated abstractions and local optimizations without establishing whether the underlying work is necessary. AI amplifies this tendency, but the problem predates AI.

    Humans are worse at writing code and performing implementation-heavy engineering than we like to admit. You need to understand the system, but you also need to move beyond manually doing everything yourself.

    real value lies in critical thinking . Just works on computing

    i genuinely think cold emailing founders is one of the most underutilized career strategies in tech.

    most people spend months optimizing resumes, applying through job boards, and competing with hundreds of candidates for the same opening.

    meanwhile, a startup just raised $10m, has 12 employees, and needs someone who can solve a problem they haven’t even written a job description for yet.

    that’s the opportunity.

    don’t ask whether they’re hiring. figure out what they’re building, where the hard problems are, and why you’re unusually qualified to solve one of them.

    then email the founder with evidence. a project, a technical investigation, a concrete idea. something that makes the conversation about your ability, not your credentials.

    the best part? you don’t need to be the most qualified person on paper. you need to be the person who makes the strongest case for why you belong there.

    At the highest level, writing is part of engineering.

    distinction is between writing that adds cognitive structure and writing that merely adds volume.

    Writing helps you avoid getting lost in bad architecture. You think before you execute. Just by writing things down, you can reason about them before implementation and might not notice certain problems initially, but once you write the system down, you can see how decisions affect downstream components and create cascading effects.

    Writing forces you to make implicit ideas explicit. Once they're explicit, you can see relationships, contradictions, dependencies, invariants, and downstream consequences that are difficult to see when everything exists only in your head.

    You need to think, write, read, challenge your assumptions, and compress the system into clearer models. Be the best writer you can be in engineering.
